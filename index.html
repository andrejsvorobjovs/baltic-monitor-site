What this is — and isn't

What it does

Scans public news, official government sources, and structured event data for Baltic/Russia security signals, then scores results by how many independent sources corroborate the same story before flagging anything as a real alert.

What it is not

Not an official government, NATO, or intelligence source, and not affiliated with any of them. Not verified intelligence or a predictor of the future — a keyword-and-corroboration score, built and run by one person. It can miss things, and it can occasionally be wrong.

Method

How the signal is scored

Every source sits in a trust tier. No single source, however alarming, can trigger a real alert alone — only independent corroboration across tiers does.

Before you ask

Common questions

What if you get something wrong?

It gets logged and published as a correction, not quietly fixed — the count above is the full record. Occasionally being wrong and saying so openly is more trustworthy than a suspiciously spotless record.

Do you see who's following this?

No — Telegram channels don't show the admin who subscribed, only a total count. This page and the channel don't collect or store personal data about you.

Will this stay free?

Yes. If a donation option is ever added, it won't buy faster or better alerts — everyone gets exactly the same thing, at the same time, regardless.

Is this an official warning system?

No. If you're worried about an actual emergency, always check your own country's official channels first: Estonia — kriis.ee · Latvia — vugd.gov.lv · Lithuania — lrv.lt.

Baltic Signal Monitor is an independent, personally-built monitoring tool. It is not an official government, NATO, or intelligence source, and is not affiliated with any of them. Corrections are logged and published openly, not hidden.
